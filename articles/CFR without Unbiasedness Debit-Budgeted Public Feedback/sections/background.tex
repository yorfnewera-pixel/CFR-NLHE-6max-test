\section{Deterministic Public Feedback as a Control Problem}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Game, local rows, and output.}
Fix a finite two-player zero-sum perfect-recall game with player-zero payoff
$u_0=u=-u_1$.  Let $x$ and $y$ be realization plans and define
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{expl}(x,y)=\tfrac12\left[
\max_{x'}u(x',y)-\min_{y'}u(x,y')\right].
\label{eq:gap-definition}
\end{equation}
For information set $I$ owned by player $i$, write $\rho=(i,I)$ for its local
row and $p_{\rho,t}$ for its behavior at round $t$.  If
$v_{\rho,t}$ is the counterfactual action-value vector, its centered feedback
is $v_{\rho,t}-\langle p_{\rho,t},v_{\rho,t}\rangle\one$.  Additive signed
regret matching (RM) accumulates centered rows; additive RM+ clips the updated
state coordinatewise at zero \citep{tammelin2014cfrplus}.  In both cases,
regret matching normalizes the positive part of the state and uses the
uniform distribution when its positive mass is zero, matching the solver
implementation.  We use the native
alternating recurrence
$(x_t,y_t)\to(x_{t+1},y_t)\to(x_{t+1},y_{t+1})$ and predetermined
nonnegative nondecreasing output weights $w_t$.  Put
$W_T=\sum_{t=1}^T w_t>0$ and denote the weighted pre/pre average by
$(\bar x_T^w,\bar y_T^w)$.

\paragraph{Finite additive public cut.}
Let $J$ contain $N$ semantic public labels.  For row $\rho$, label $j$, and
the immutable consuming profile at event $t$, let
$X_{\rho,t,j}$ be the centered contribution of label $j$.  A width-$B$ plan,
where $B$ divides $N$, selects $S_t\subseteq J$ with $|S_t|=B$ and delivers
\begin{equation}
g_{\rho,t}=\sum_{j\in J}X_{\rho,t,j},\qquad
h_{\rho,t}=\frac{N}{B}\sum_{j\in S_t}X_{\rho,t,j}.
\label{eq:public-rows}
\end{equation}
Here $g_{\rho,t}$ is the dense full-cut target and $h_{\rho,t}$ is the row
consumed by the learner.  Exactly evaluated terms outside the public cut are
shared by both rows and cancel from their difference.  The appendix extends
the notation to finitely many nonnested cuts and proves the additive
decomposition from terminal-history linearity.

Define the row error
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\rho,t}=h_{\rho,t}-g_{\rho,t}
\label{eq:row-error}
\end{equation}
and, for any finite vector $z$, the one-sided underestimation gauge
\begin{equation}
\psi(z)=\pos{\max_a\{-z(a)\}}=\norm{[-z]_+}_\infty.
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
If $z$ is coordinatewise nonnegative, delivered cumulative feedback dominates
the target and $\psi(z)=0$, even when $z\ne0$.  During player zero's update,
define the target-payoff decrease
\begin{equation}
d_t=\pos{u(x_t,y_t)-u(x_{t+1},y_t)}.
\label{eq:target-decrease}
\end{equation}
The public part of a block's exact target debit is the sum of the row gauges;
the alternating part is the weighted sum of $d_t$.

The common feedback regimes use different guarantees.  Complete coverage is
exact at every origin, and independent public sampling is target valid through
conditional expectation.  A persistent partial order instead balances counts
across events with different learner origins; its connection to
$g_{\rho,t}$ must therefore be established pathwise.

\paragraph{Control decision and event-faithful execution.}
At a completed block boundary, a candidate plan $a$ fixes a common number $m$
of alternating rounds, a width $B$, a balanced public order, and an admissible
physical executor.  The planner selects a plan from completed history and a
settled debit account.  The selected plan executes once; its target-debit
charge may be certified before execution or settled by an audit after the
block.  Thus width and order are learner decisions, while processor placement
is permitted only when it preserves the declared event record.

All selected components of one learner event must use one immutable profile
and join in a deterministic canonical order.  All rows owned by the updating
player read the resulting common delivered payoff functional and update
simultaneously in exactly one learner transition.  We call this contract
\emph{event-faithful}.  Physical
parallelism inside the join may change runtime but not the declared learner
trajectory.  Complete coverage is the plan $B=N$, for which
$h_{\rho,t}=g_{\rho,t}$ at every event.
